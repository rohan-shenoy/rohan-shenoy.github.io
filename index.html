I am currently a PhD student in Computer and Information Science at the University of Pennsylvania, where I am advised by Professors Linh Thi Xuan Phan and Benjamin Lee. I am fortunate to be funded by the NSF Graduate Research Fellowship.

My research focuses on the systems that robots run on, spanning distributed, embedded, and runtime systems. I am also interested in sustainable computing from a systems perspective: designing infrastructure that reduces the energy and carbon cost of computation without sacrificing performance.

Before Penn, I studied Computer Science and Applied Mathematics at UC Berkeley. As an undergraduate, I spent two years with the MIT Climate and Sustainability Consortium, advised by Elsa Olivetti and Evan Coleman, where I used reinforcement learning to speed up NMR pulse sequencing for measuring soil atomic abundance. I have also collaborated with researchers at UMass Amherst, MIT, and Caltech on carbon-aware resource management, from scheduling large data processing workloads in the cloud to running machine learning efficiently on edge devices.

Outside of research, I enjoy skiing, soccer (as a dedicated Bayern Munich fan), backgammon and other strategy games, and puzzles.
